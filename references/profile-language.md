# How strong profiles are actually written

Sixteen public profiles read through the connector on 26 September 2026, four per
lane: GoHighLevel and CRM, Make and n8n, AI chatbots and agents, WordPress. Badges
ranged from none to Top Rated Plus, earnings from $80 to $200,000, rates from $6 to
$50. A candidate sample, not a ranking: without a client account there is no way to
filter by earnings or Job Success, so strength is read off badge and earnings bucket.

Patterns only. No profile text was copied, and a member who copies one of these
sentences competes with everyone else who did.

**What this sample cannot show:** the connector returns no portfolio array, so how
portfolio items are titled stays unmeasured here. Use
[profile-formula.md](profile-formula.md) for that.

## The title, 16 of 16

55 to 70 characters, so the practical ceiling gets used. Pipes in 12 of 16, the rest
using commas, hyphens, tildes or a prepositional phrase. Two to five blocks, almost
always the same shape: **tool, then a role noun, then a deliverable noun**. Verbs
appear once in sixteen. A number appears once, a badge word twice, and a price or a
number of years never.

## The rhythm

One line is one sentence of 6 to 14 words. Paragraphs run 1 to 3 sentences.

The exception is the interesting part: **the two highest earners, at $100,000 and
$200,000 plus, write real prose** with 25 to 40 word sentences and almost no emoji,
while checkmark lists correlate with the lower earnings in this sample. Lists are
what a beginner reaches for, and they are not what the top of this sample does.

## The perspective flips twice

Hook in "you" and "your", middle in "I build" and "I help", close as an imperative
back to "you". Twelve of sixteen hinge on the same word, "let's".

The measured ratio is less flattering: roughly 15% of the text is the reader's
problem, 70% the freelancer's own abilities, 10% the reader again. Only two profiles
hold the reader's side throughout, one through FAQ-style headings and one by giving
each target audience its own section.

## Four ways a section opens

A bare heading, dominant across the sample. A pain sentence. A question. An outcome
promise. **The pain sentence is what separates the three strongest GoHighLevel and
automation profiles** from the rest, and it is also the cheapest thing for a beginner
to write well, because it needs no history.

## How objections get handled

Three moves. A negation list, for example no duplicated workflows and no overloaded
pipelines. FAQ-style headings, used once and by the most complete profile in the
sample. And a reliability promise. Eleven of sixteen answer "it will not break" and
six answer "you will own and understand it".

## The verbs

**Build** is the backbone in all sixteen. Then automate, convert, capture, scale,
integrate, qualify, nurture. **Fix** appears only where repair is the product, in
GoHighLevel and WordPress.

## What the lanes do differently

**GoHighLevel and CRM:** hook, a service list with checkmarks, a "why clients hire
me" block, then the ask. Proof is mostly a checkmark claim; one named case with a
number across four profiles. Skills all twenty slots filled, around HighLevel, CRM
Automation, Marketing Automation, Sales Funnel, Lead Generation.

**Make and n8n**, the hardest lane: all four sell **robustness rather than features**,
naming error handling, retries, logging, documentation and handover. Two carry a
"recent builds" block with hours or percentages. Skills around n8n, Make.com, Zapier,
API Integration, AI Agent Development.

**AI chatbots and agents:** the only lane where Unicode bold headers are the norm and
credentials come first, including a PhD and a "top 1%" claim. No profile opens with a
question; all open by placing themselves. The client's pain is replaced by a tech
stack: LangChain, RAG, vector databases.

**WordPress:** the only lane with a real process block, numbered one to five, in three
of four, and with a tool list as a trust signal, naming WP Rocket, Cloudflare,
RankMath. Skills around WordPress Development, Elementor, WooCommerce, Page Speed
Optimization.

## Pricing and the close

**Nobody names a price or a package.** Two offer a free call, one offers a paid first
session whose deliverable is a written plan to keep, one promises a fixed price within
24 hours.

Fourteen of sixteen close with an imperative plus permission, of the shape "send me
what you want to automate, even if it is messy". **Not one closes by talking about
itself.**

## What nobody in the sample does

No prices. No disqualification criteria, so no "this is not for you if". No
availability window. No date marker on any claim. No numeric guarantee, with one
exception promising a PageSpeed score above 90. And no profile made of plain text
without a single checkmark, except the two that write prose.

Read that list twice: every item on it is free, and each one is a way to be the only
profile in a search that does it.

## The warning in the data

Two of sixteen claim "Top Rated" or "100% Job Success" or "250+ clients" in their
overview text while the connector's own aggregate shows no badge and ten or five
completed jobs. **A badge named in an overview is not evidence.** `/audit` judges the
real badge and aggregate, never the sentence, and `/profile` never writes a claim this
member cannot back with its own file.
