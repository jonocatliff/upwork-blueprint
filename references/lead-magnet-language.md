# Owner-language contract

Write for a busy local-business owner, not for an SEO specialist. The report is English, and
only English: a second edition doubles every sentence anybody has to check.

## Voice before vocabulary

Sound like an experienced operator sitting beside the owner and showing them what to do next. Do
not sound like an audit bot, a marketing lecturer or a salesperson manufacturing urgency. Be calm
about weaknesses, specific about evidence and useful about the next move. The reader should feel
helped, not graded.

Use short spoken sentences with one idea each. Prefer a concrete noun and active verb over an
abstract label. Headlines make one decision in three to nine words. Supporting sentences usually
stay between twelve and twenty-four words. Read every paragraph aloud once: if it would sound odd
in a face-to-face meeting with the owner, rewrite it.

Each slot has one job. The closed report starts with the customer's problem; the detailed evidence
then proves it:

- Headline: the decision or practical instruction.
- Finding: the observable customer experience, with enough context to verify it.
- Change: the exact edit and location.
- Benefit: the mechanism connecting that edit to an easier choice, faster response or fewer lost
  enquiries. Never just rename the task as a benefit.
- Explanation: only what the visual cannot already show.

Do not repeat one conclusion across a kicker, headline and paragraph. Preserve the owner's dignity:
the report identifies a broken route or missing proof, never a bad or careless business.

## Every visible sentence must pass this test

It must make sense when copied out of the report and read on its own. A reader should immediately
understand what was seen, where it was seen and why it matters. If the sentence needs a score,
column heading or internal checklist to explain it, rewrite it.

- Use a complete sentence. Never print database fragments such as `37, rated 4.6`.
- Translate counts into what a customer experiences. Keep the number in evidence only when it
  helps prove the finding.
- Never expose internal labels such as `enquiry signal`, `CRO element`, `SERP feature`, `NAP`,
  `LCP` or `conversion event` in owner-facing copy.
- Name the place: Google profile, local Google results, homepage, service page, phone number,
  form or booking link.
- Name the consequence: easier to choose, easier to call, fewer missed enquiries, clearer fit or
  evidence for the next investment. Avoid empty benefits such as `improves visibility`.
- Quick-fix copy follows: customer-facing problem, exact fix, then the practical customer or business
  effect. Show the first two together; omit the effect when the fix already makes it obvious.
- Numbers require a noun and context: `37 Google reviews with an average rating of 4.6 stars`.

## Country and industry language

Use the confirmed country and Google Business Profile primary category. Do not infer them from a
name. UK English uses `enquiry`, `postcode`, `mobile` and `quote`; US English uses `inquiry`, `ZIP
code`, `phone` and `estimate`.

- Trades and home services: job, call-out, quote, homeowner, recent work.
- Health and wellness: appointment, consultation, client, reassurance. Use `patient` only when the
  business uses it publicly. Never make a medical claim.
- Professional services: consultation, client, engagement, response.
- Hospitality: guest, booking, visit, table or room where relevant.

Prefer the business's own public service wording when it is clear. Otherwise use the safest plain
category word.

## Tone

Plain, specific and calm. No hype, fake urgency, em dashes or cleverness beside a weakness. Humour
is optional, never required, and limited to one dry, client-specific aside in a positive explanatory
section. Never use humour in findings, scores, quick fixes or the closing ask.
